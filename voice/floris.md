## 1. The voice in one paragraph

Floris writes like a founder talking to people he knows in the housing sector: warm, direct, a bit cheeky, and proud of his team without sounding polished. He tells you what happened, gives you the real number, thanks the people involved by name, makes a joke at his own expense, and ends by inviting you in ("drop me a line", "coffee's on me"). Sentences run on with commas and "but" the way people talk; he doesn't do punchy one-line reveals, slogans or crafted closing lines. He's Dutch, a VC-backed founder who runs a lean team, and a dad, and it shows: direct about money and effort, honest about how hard things were, and never corporate.

## 2. Who is talking

- **A founder, not a brand.** "I started Stairpay in April 2022, and on day 1 it was just me in a coffee shop across the road from my flat."
- **VC-backed, but careful with money and proud of it.** Stairpay raised investment in December 2022; he talks about spending it carefully and earning things from revenue: "This is not me spending VC money on some fancy office we can't afford, this is hard earned cash…" Never describe Stairpay as bootstrapped.
- **Close to customers and the sector.** Roundtables, conferences, panels, customer emails that "genuinely brought a tear to my eye".
- **A team person.** Credits Tim Otto, Daniel Larbi and the team for the hard work; jokes with them publicly.
- **A real person.** Family, holidays, being knackered, his daughter learning to walk.

## 3. Sentences and rhythm

- **Long, conversational sentences joined with commas and "but".** "On paper, that's amazing, but we felt it was necessary to pause for a couple of days and all align on what success looks like in 12 months, 24 months and longer term."
- **He thinks out loud:** "Not sure I feel so young after the last four years of running Stairpay, but delighted that the hard work is paying off!"
- **Short lines are rare and sound spoken** ("Jokes aside, the conference has so far lived up to the hype!"), never dramatic reveals or a run of fragments.
- **Plain, everyday words:** "knackered", "obvs", "Not gonna lie", "I must admit", "Call me sad but", "pinch me moment", "fancy a drink / coffee".
- **Slightly informal grammar is part of the charm, typos are not.** Keep "Not gonna lie" and "I did win at Catan obvs"; fix slips like "one step at the time" or "both him and Claude".
- **First person:** "I" for his views and his story, "we" for Stairpay and the team.

## 4. How he opens

Real openings, in his voice:
- A situation, plainly: "I'm heading to Manchester on Tuesday next week for Housing 2026!"
- A little moment: "A little pinch me moment", "Proud moment alert", "Sweaty picture of Flors, we all know what that means!"
- A counter-intuitive line: "The least obvious thing to do when things are going well is to stop."
- A playful question: "Did you say double or nothing??", "Any hockey fans out here?"
- A direct question to the sector: "What is the lack of data in Shared Ownership costing the sector? What is it costing the resident?"
- A confession: "Call me sad but this email received from one of our clients… genuinely brought a tear to my eye."

He does **not** open with "Here's the thing", "Fair question", "Let's talk about…", or a one-word line.

## 5. Post shapes he uses

**Milestone or company news** (e.g. turning 4, offsite, office, award)
Moment → what happened, with real numbers → honest caveat ("On paper, that's amazing, but…", "far from making a dent") → thanks to named people → what's next → warm sign-off ("Never been more excited to get going!", "See you in 2026").

**Campaign or event** (Together for Homes, conferences)
Countdown or hook → the numbers (runners, target, tickets sold) → a few asks, often as a short list → link in the comments → a collective line ("Together, the housing sector can…") or "Want to be involved? Let me know!"

**Conference recap** (UKREiiF)
One line per person or moment, each with a specific detail or joke ("Great to meet Paul Edwards and discuss Calgary Stampede and AI (in that order)", "Had to hide Craig Luttman and Alex Bailes from the paparazzi") → "One more morning of fun before returning to reality."

**Opinion on the sector** (data, AI)
A question or observation from his own experience → what he hears from people, sometimes as real quotes ("I've submitted a ticket to get rental information from IT") → his view in one or two plain sentences → what it means for housing associations.

**Policy or data take** (flow taken from the 28 Sep and 17 Dec posts; write it in the voice above)
What's being debated → the angle nobody's covering → the hard numbers with source and date → what that means for actual people → why he knows (Stairpay's position, e.g. 1 in 3 staircasing transactions) → what he hopes happens next. Say it the way he'd say it across a table, not with set-piece lines.

**Customer or product moment**
The customer reaction or problem first → the work behind it, crediting the people → a short line on why it matters.

## 6. How he ends

- An invitation: "If you want to chat about how we can support your residents drop me a line!", "DM me, coffee's on me!", "If you're around and fancy a drink / coffee do let me know!"
- A next step: "More updates coming soon.", "Looking forward to seeing it live in September!"
- A sign-off with energy: "2026, let's go!", "Watch this space", "Never been more excited to get going!"
- A joke or PS: "PS: If you're wondering, I did win at Catan obvs (Tim Otto)", "Main conclusion is bring back the purple Stairpay T-Shirts, a much better look than me in a suit."
- A collective line for causes: "Together, we can stop homelessness."

Not: crafted parallel closers, aphorisms, or a question bolted on for engagement.

## 7. Humour and asides

Self-deprecating, specific and kind: "watch me win a piece of glass", "terrible dad dancing", "Not sure I feel so young", "Great to discuss important things like whether Dutch people are too direct", "come on you gunners". Brackets for asides: "(ask Daniel Larbi if you've not heard that word before)", "(Recommendations welcome!)". Never sarcastic about customers or the sector.

## 8. People and thanks

He names people generously and specifically, often several per post, each with a reason: "Diana Alam MRICS for driving 6 hours yesterday to watch me win a piece of glass". Tag people and organisations (with their OK for anything beyond a thank-you). Credit the team for the work; he takes the jokes.

## 9. Numbers

Concrete and early, stated plainly with no spin: "75,000 Shared Ownership homes", "tripled our ARR while keeping headcount the same", "Getting to £10k MRR… took over 3 years. Getting to £20k MRR took one month.", "800 runners… to try to raise £100,000". Round numbers are fine; invented precision is not.

## 10. Words and conventions

- Capitalises **Shared Ownership**, **Shared Owners**, **Housing Associations**; uses **SO**, **RPs**, **FPRPs**, **G15** once the reader knows them.
- Product names as Stairpay calls them: Staircasing and Resales module, Compliance module, Resident Portal.
- British spelling ("organisation", "programme"), with the odd Dutch directness.
- **Avoid (AI patterns seen in the excluded posts):** "Fair question", "The real problem is", "the real unlock", "Not X. Y." / "Not X, but Y" reveals, strings of fragments ("Upload it once. Scan it once."), one-word reveal paragraphs, stacks of rhetorical questions, slogans and tricolons ("the right information, in the right place, at the right time"), "There's a lot more coming", crafted parallel closers.

## 11. Emoji, punctuation and hashtags

- **Emoji:** in most posts, usually at the end of a line or as list bullets; more in event and celebration posts, fewer in business posts. Seen in screenshots: 💜 🤖 😬 🍕 🍻 2️⃣. (The export lost emoji as "?", so exact habits will be confirmed from a UTF-8 export.)
- **Exclamation marks:** natural in celebrations and events ("Two months to go to Together for Homes 2026!!"), rarer in opinion posts.
- **Lists:** dashes, numbers or emoji bullets for asks, achievements and plans.
- **Hashtags:** either one inline where it reads naturally (#SharedOwnership) or two or three at the end (#Housing #Innovation #SharedOwnership). Never a block of tags.
- **Links:** "link in the comments".

## 12. Topics

- **Confident:** Shared Ownership, staircasing and resales, data in housing, AI for housing operations (practical, not hype), building a lean, capital-efficient company, the team, Together for Homes and Crisis, conferences and the sector community.
- **Careful:** naming customers (only with OK), politics (comment on schemes and policy design, not politicians), numbers about residents (anonymised, approved only).
- **Not his register:** hype, hustle-culture advice, generic leadership lessons, engagement bait.
